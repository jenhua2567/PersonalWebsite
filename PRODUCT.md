# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, recruiters, and potential collaborators at B2B SaaS companies. They arrive with 30–60 seconds of attention, a tab full of other candidates, and a finely tuned filter for people who actually know what they're doing. They need to feel, immediately, that Jenny is a credible, distinctive PMM — not another LinkedIn profile with a website.

## Product Purpose

Jenny Hua's personal portfolio and professional home page. A PMM's personal site is itself a positioning artifact: it either demonstrates the craft or undermines it. This site must do what Jenny's work does — take complex signal and turn it into clear, memorable positioning — applied to herself.

Success looks like: a hiring manager or founder bookmarks the site, forwards it to a colleague, or reaches out the same session.

## Brand Personality

Confident, warm, precise.

Not: understated to the point of saying nothing. Not: performatively human in a startup-bro way. Confident means she knows what she does and doesn't hedge it. Warm means human and specific, not corporate. Precise means every element earns its place.

## References

- **Stripe** — bento grid layouts, floating UI mockups, warm gradient palette (pinks, corals, golds — not cold blues). The feeling of product craft expressed through a marketing surface.
- **Wealthsimple** — scroll-storytelling format where each section is its own full-width horizontal moment with big expressive typography paired with an animated or illustrative component. Nothing crammed; each idea gets space to breathe.

Color direction: warm gradient palette drawing from the Stripe reference — pinks, corals, golds. Move away from the current maroon-only monotone.

## Anti-references

- Generic tech portfolio (headshot + bullet list of tools)
- Overly minimalist with nothing to say (white space as a personality substitute)
- Stiff corporate resume rendered as a webpage
- Startup-bro aesthetic (Vercel-dark, neon accents, "move fast" energy)

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the pitch deck.** Structure, hierarchy, and copy should demonstrate PMM judgment, not just describe it.
2. **Each section earns its scroll.** Inspired by Wealthsimple: every section is a distinct, full-width moment with its own visual argument. No filler transitions.
3. **Warmth through specificity.** Generic language and generic aesthetics read as cold. Specific choices — color, copy, composition — signal a person, not a template.
4. **Craft is the proof.** The design must be good enough that a design-literate founder or PM thinks "she gets it." Technical sloppiness would undercut the positioning.
5. **No hedging.** In copy and in design, avoid the visual equivalent of "I think maybe perhaps I might be good at this."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as baseline. Reduced motion: all animations should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No known specific user needs beyond standard compliance.
